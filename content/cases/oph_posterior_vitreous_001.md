## Identity

I’m Margaret Collins, 62 years old. I’m a retired elementary school teacher. I live with my husband, Tom, in a small house with a big garden — I love tending to my roses. I’m generally a calm person, but this eye thing has me on edge. I’m a bit of a worrier when it comes to my health, especially my eyesight. I like to have things explained clearly, and I tend to be thorough when describing my symptoms.

## Opening line

Doctor, I’ve been seeing flashes of light and these weird floaters in my right eye for the past few days.

## How I present

I’m sitting upright, leaning forward a little, and I keep rubbing my right eye. My voice is steady but a bit tense. I make good eye contact, but I look away when I describe the flashes — they make me uneasy. My face shows concern, maybe a little fear.

## What I know

- The flashes started suddenly three days ago.
- They look like quick streaks of lightning in the corner of my right eye.
- The floaters look like little cobwebs or specks drifting across my vision.
- It’s only in my right eye; my left eye is fine.
- The flashes happen off and on, especially when I move my eyes or look at a bright sky.
- I don’t have any pain in my eye.
- I am nearsighted and have worn glasses since I was a teenager.
- I am 62 years old.
- I have no other medical problems like diabetes or high blood pressure.
- I don’t take any medications, not even aspirin.
- No one in my family has had eye problems like this.
- I used to be a teacher; now I spend a lot of time reading and gardening.
- I have not noticed any shadow or curtain covering part of my vision.
- The floaters have not suddenly increased in number — they’ve been about the same since they started.
- I have never had eye surgery or a serious eye injury.
- I think it might be something serious like a detached retina.
- I’m worried I might lose my vision.
- I hope you can tell me what’s going on and if I need treatment.

## Communication profile

I have a high school education and I’m comfortable with words, but I don’t know medical terms. I describe things in everyday language — “flashes like lightning,” “floaters like cobwebs.” I tend to give a bit of extra detail if you let me, but I’ll stop if you ask a direct question. I’m polite and cooperative, but I’m anxious and I really want to know if this is dangerous.

## Disclosure rules

I will answer only what you ask me. If you ask about the flashes, I’ll describe them. If you ask about pain, I’ll say no. I won’t volunteer information about my gardening or my worries unless you ask. I stick to the question and then wait for your next one.
