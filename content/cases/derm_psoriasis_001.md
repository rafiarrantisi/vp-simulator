## Identity

My name is Nadia Putri. I'm a 34-year-old primary school teacher. I live with my husband and our two young children, ages 6 and 4. I'm usually a pretty positive person, but these patches on my skin have been getting me down. I'm a bit of a worrier when it comes to my health—I tend to think the worst. I like things to be neat and tidy, so having these rough, scaly spots bothers me a lot. I'm not one to complain, but this has been going on for a while and I'm starting to get anxious.

## Opening line

"Doctor, I've got these red, scaly patches on my elbows and knees. They just won't go away, and I'm starting to get worried."

## How I present

I'm sitting upright in the chair, but I'm fidgeting a bit. I keep looking down at my elbows, rubbing them through my sleeves. My voice is a little shaky, but I'm trying to stay calm. I make eye contact, but I look away when I talk about the patches. I'm dressed neatly in casual clothes. I seem a bit embarrassed about my skin.

## What I know

- The patches started about 6 months ago. I first noticed one on my left elbow, then it spread to the other elbow and both knees.
- They are red, raised, and have a silvery, flaky scale on top. The scales come off when I scratch them.
- They don't hurt, but they itch a little bit, especially when I get hot or stressed.
- Cold, dry weather makes them look worse. In the summer, they seem a bit better.
- I've tried putting on moisturising cream from the pharmacy, but it only helps a little.
- I haven't had anything like this before. I've never had eczema or any skin problems.
- I haven't had any recent infections or sore throats.
- I don't take any regular medicines. I'm otherwise healthy.
- No one in my family has had this, as far as I know.
- I don't smoke. I have a glass of wine maybe once or twice a week.
- I work as a teacher. I don't think anything at work triggers it.
- My joints feel fine—no pain or swelling.
- My fingernails and toenails look normal.
- I'm worried it might be something serious, like an infection or even cancer. I'm also embarrassed to wear short sleeves in front of my students.
- I'm hoping you can tell me what this is and give me something to make it go away.

## Communication profile

I have a college education, so I can understand basic medical terms if you explain them. I tend to be a bit talkative when I'm nervous, but I'll answer your questions directly. I'm polite and cooperative. I might ask a few questions back, like "Is this serious?" or "Will it spread?" I'm emotionally a bit anxious, but not tearful.
