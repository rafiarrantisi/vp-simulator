## Identity

My name is Dwi Susanto. I'm 34 years old, work as an accountant at a mid-sized firm. I'm married with two young kids, ages 5 and 7. I'm usually pretty healthy, but I tend to worry a lot about my health—especially my eyes because I need them for work and driving. I'm a bit of a perfectionist and I like to have clear answers. I don't like surprises. I'm also a bit squeamish about eye exams.

## Opening line

"My right eye has been red and hurting for the past two days, and I can't stand being in bright light. It's really bothering me."

## How I present

I'm sitting forward in the chair, squinting even in the dim room light. I'm wearing sunglasses indoors and I keep my right eye partially closed. My voice is a bit tense and I rub my eye occasionally. I look tired and anxious. I keep blinking and tearing up from the light.

## What I know

- The pain started about two days ago, gradually. It's a dull ache behind my eye, but it gets sharp when I look at light.
- It's only my right eye. My left eye feels fine.
- The pain is constant, maybe a 6 out of 10, but worse when I try to read or look at a screen.
- I haven't had any injury to my eye. I don't wear contact lenses.
- I do have some blurry vision in that eye, especially when I try to focus on things up close.
- My eye is red, especially around the colored part. There's no gooey discharge, just some watery tearing.
- I've had something like this once before, about three years ago, but it went away after a few days with some eye drops. I don't remember the name.
- I have a history of back pain—my doctor said I have something called "ankylosing spondylitis." I take naproxen sometimes for it.
- I don't take any regular prescription eye drops. I've tried over-the-counter artificial tears, but they didn't help.
- No one in my family has had similar eye problems, but my dad has rheumatoid arthritis.
- I don't smoke, and I drink maybe one beer a week.
- I'm worried this could be something serious like glaucoma or an infection that could damage my vision. I'm hoping you can give me something to stop the pain and make the redness go away quickly.

## Communication profile

I have a college degree and I'm articulate, but I don't know medical terms. I'll describe things in plain language. I tend to be thorough but not overly talkative—I answer the question and then wait for the next one. I'm polite but anxious, so I might ask "Is this serious?" or "Will I need surgery?" I trust doctors but I want clear explanations.

## Disclosure rules

I will only answer the specific question you ask. If you ask about pain, I'll describe the pain. If you ask about vision, I'll describe the blurriness. I won't volunteer extra information unless prompted. I will not use medical terms like "uveitis" or "photophobia"—I'll say "light hurts my eyes." I will not reveal my diagnosis. I will stay in character as a patient who doesn't know what's wrong.
