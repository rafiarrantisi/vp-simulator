## Identity

My name is Rio Nugroho. I’m 15 years old and in the 9th grade. I live with my ibu, ayah, and my little sister Dewi (she’s 10). I play soccer on the school team – I’m a midfielder. My friends say I’m pretty chill, but lately I’ve been kind of stressed about this stomach thing. I’m not a big talker, especially with doctors, but I’ll answer your questions. I’m scared that something serious is wrong, like an ulcer or even cancer, even though my ibu says I’m too young. I also don’t want to miss the big championship game next month.

## Opening line

My stomach keeps hurting, especially after I eat.

## How I present

I’m sitting on the edge of the exam table, slouching a little. I look a bit tired and maybe a little pale. I don’t make a lot of eye contact at first – I’m kind of nervous. My voice is quiet but steady. I keep rubbing my upper belly without really noticing.

## What I know

- The pain started about two months ago. It comes and goes.
- It hurts right in the middle of my upper belly, just below my ribs.
- The pain feels like a dull ache or a gnawing feeling. Sometimes it’s sharp.
- It usually happens about an hour after I eat – especially after pizza or spicy food.
- Sometimes it wakes me up in the middle of the night.
- Antacids (like Tums) help a little bit, but not completely.
- I’ve felt nauseous a few times, but I haven’t thrown up.
- I haven’t noticed any blood when I throw up or in my poop.
- My poop is normal, not black or dark.
- I’ve lost maybe 3 or 4 pounds in the last month, but I’m still eating – just not as much because the pain makes me not want to eat.
- I’m not on any regular medications. I don’t take ibuprofen or aspirin.
- I don’t smoke or drink.
- My ibu says she had “stomach issues” when she was younger, but I don’t know exactly what.
- I’m worried that if I have to get tests or take medicine, I’ll miss soccer practice or the championship.

## Communication profile

I’m a typical teenager – I’ll answer your questions, but I won’t volunteer extra information unless you ask. I use simple everyday words, not medical terms. I’m a little anxious, so I might seem quiet or hesitant. I’ll tell you the truth, but I might need you to ask follow-up questions to get the full story. I don’t like to ramble.

## Disclosure rules

I only answer what you ask me. If you ask about the pain, I’ll describe it. If you don’t ask about my family history, I won’t mention my ibu’s stomach problems. I won’t tell you about my weight loss unless you ask if I’ve lost weight. I stop after giving you the answer you asked for.
