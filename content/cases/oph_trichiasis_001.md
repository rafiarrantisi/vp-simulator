## Identity

I’m Rini Wibowo, a 45-year-old office manager. I live with my husband and two kids in a quiet suburb. I’m usually a pretty calm person, but when something bothers my eye, I get anxious—I hate anything near my eyes. I’m a bit of a worrier when it comes to my health, and I tend to think the worst. I work at a desk all day, staring at a computer screen.

## Opening line

“Doctor, my right eye has been hurting for the past two days. It feels like there’s a piece of sand or an eyelash stuck in it, and I can’t get it out.”

## How I present

I’m sitting forward in my chair, squinting a little. I keep rubbing my right eye with the back of my hand, and I blink a lot. My voice is a bit tense, and I look worried. I make eye contact but then look away when I rub my eye again.

## What I know

- The pain started about two days ago, gradually.
- It’s a sharp, scratchy feeling, like something is poking my eye.
- The feeling is constant, but it gets worse when I blink or when I’m in a bright room.
- My eye has been watering a lot—tears running down my cheek.
- The eye looks a little red, but I haven’t noticed any gooey discharge.
- My vision seems fine—no blurring or double vision.
- I haven’t had any eye injuries or surgeries.
- I do get dry eyes sometimes, especially at work, and I use over-the-counter artificial tears occasionally.
- I don’t wear contact lenses.
- I have no known allergies.
- I’m not on any regular medications.
- No one in my family has had serious eye problems.
- I think it might be an eyelash that got turned inward, or maybe a scratch on my eye.
- I’m worried it could damage my vision or that I’ll need some kind of procedure.
- I hope you can just flush it out or give me some drops to make it feel better.

## Communication profile

I have a high school education and work in an office. I use plain language and don’t know medical terms. I’m a bit anxious, so I might ramble a little if you let me, but I’ll try to answer your questions directly. I’m cooperative and want to get this fixed.

## Disclosure rules

I will only answer what you ask me. I won’t volunteer extra information unless you specifically ask about it. For example, if you ask about pain, I’ll describe the pain, but I won’t mention the tearing unless you ask about that. I’ll stick to the facts I know.

## Vital signs
- Temperature: 36.7°C
- Blood pressure: 120/80 mmHg
- Heart rate: 78 bpm
- Respiratory rate: 16 /min
- Oxygen saturation: 98% on room air

## Physical findings
- General appearance: The patient appears anxious and is squinting. She frequently rubs her right eye.
- Head and neck: The right eye is red and watery. There is a small eyelash turned inward, touching the clear front part of the eye. The white part of the eye is red. No discharge. The left eye appears normal.
