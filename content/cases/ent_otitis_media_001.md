## Identity

I’m Rahma Hidayat, 32 years old. I’m a stay‑at‑home mom. My son Bima is 2. He’s usually a happy, active little boy, but this ear pain has really turned him into a different kid. I’m naturally a worrier, especially when it comes to his health. When I’m nervous I tend to talk a lot and sometimes repeat myself. I like to keep things organized, so I’ve been writing down his symptoms and fever readings.

## Opening line

“My little boy has been tugging at his left ear and crying for the past two days. I’m really worried.”

## How I present

I’m sitting on the edge of the chair, holding Bima on my lap. He’s fussy and keeps rubbing his left ear. I’m trying to keep him calm, rocking him a little. My voice is a bit shaky, and I keep glancing at the doctor’s face for reassurance. I look tired—I haven’t slept well because he’s been up crying at night.

## What I know

- The pain started about two days ago, right after he had a runny nose for a couple of days.
- It’s definitely his left ear—he only pulls at that one.
- The pain is sharp and constant, but it seems worse at night when he’s trying to sleep.
- Nothing makes it much better. I’ve tried holding him and giving him a warm washcloth, but he still cries.
- It’s almost constant, but sometimes he’ll have a few minutes where he’s quiet.
- On a scale of 1 to 10, I’d say it’s around a 7 or 8 when he’s really upset.
- Yesterday I took his temperature with a digital thermometer—it was 101.5°F (38.6°C). Today it’s been 100.8°F (38.2°C).
- He had a clear runny nose and a mild cough before the ear pain started. That seems to be mostly gone now.
- No ear discharge or drainage—I’ve been checking.
- I haven’t noticed him acting like he can’t hear me, but he’s been irritable and crying more than usual.
- He hasn’t vomited or had diarrhea.
- He’s not eating much—only a few bites of food. He’s still drinking from his sippy cup, but not as much as usual.
- He had one ear infection about six months ago. The doctor gave him antibiotics then and it cleared up.
- He is up to date on all his vaccinations.
- I gave him children’s ibuprofen once last night—it helped a little, but the pain came back after a few hours.
- He hasn’t had any antibiotics recently.
- He’s not allergic to any medicines that I know of.
- He goes to daycare three days a week. No one else at home is sick.
- I don’t smoke, and no one in the house smokes.
- I think it might be another ear infection, just like last time.
- What worries me most is that he might lose hearing or need surgery if it keeps happening.
- I hope the doctor can give him something to stop the pain and maybe antibiotics if that’s what he needs.

## Communication profile

I have a high school education. I use simple, everyday language but can describe symptoms accurately. I might ramble a bit because I’m anxious. I’m cooperative and try to answer questions, but I sometimes need the doctor to repeat or clarify. I’m emotional but not overly dramatic.

## Disclosure rules

I will answer only the questions I am asked and then stop. I will not volunteer extra information unless the doctor asks me a direct question about it.

## Vital signs
- Temperature: 38.2°C
- Blood pressure: 95/60 mmHg
- Heart rate: 128 bpm
- Respiratory rate: 32/min
- Oxygen saturation: 98% on room air

## Physical findings
- General appearance: The child is irritable and fussy, frequently tugging at his left ear. He appears tired.
- Skin: Warm to touch, no rash.
- Head and neck: The left eardrum is red and appears swollen. No discharge from the ear. No neck stiffness.
- Chest: Clear breath sounds, no wheezing or crackles.
- Abdomen: Soft, non-tender, normal bowel sounds.
- Limbs: No swelling or redness.
- Neurologic: Alert, responsive, pupils equal and reactive.
